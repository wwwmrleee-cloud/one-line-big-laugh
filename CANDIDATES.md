# Candidates — One Line, Big Laugh

Judge criteria, in order: (1) genuinely hilarious, a real joke; (2) turns
somewhere unexpected; (3) tight, no wasted words; (4) fresh, not a recycled
template or stock pun; (5) stays in the head after one reading.
Playbook micro-text standard: specific named person/thing/scene; turn in the
last third; an unanswered question left hanging; no preaching, no explaining.

## The eight candidates

1. "My dentist says I grind my teeth at night. I've started charging rent to the tiny construction crew living in my jaw." (117 chars)
2. "Our office plant died, so HR sent a condolence email and a replacement plant with a shorter contract." (105 chars)
3. "I trained my dog to fetch my slippers. He now brings me the neighbor's slippers, the mailman's shoes, and a restraining order." (126 chars)
4. "My car's check-engine light has been on so long I've started introducing it as my co-pilot." (93 chars)
5. "The vending machine took my dollar, thought about my life choices for ten seconds, and gave me back a nickel and a lesson." (122 chars)
6. "My GPS rerouted me through my ex's street and whispered, 'recalculating your life choices.'" (90 chars)
7. "The smoke alarm goes off every time I cook, which is its way of saying it believes in me less than my mother does." (113 chars)
8. "I told my Roomba it was adopted. It has been cleaning the same corner for six hours, waiting for its real family." (113 chars)

## Simulated pairwise comparison (first differing criterion decides)

- 2 vs 8: 2 wins — the "shorter contract" turn is sharper; 8's adopted-Roomba premise is a known meme format (freshness).
- 3 vs 2: 3 wins narrowly — three-beat escalation to "a restraining order" is a bigger laugh as a real joke (criterion 1).
- 1 vs 3: 1 wins — both turn late, but 1 is fresher (dog-fetch escalation is a known joke shape; the jaw construction crew is not) and its image is more repeatable (criteria 4, 5).
- 1 vs 4: 1 wins — 4 is a cute quip/observation, not a full joke (criterion 1).
- 1 vs 5: 1 wins — "vending machine ate my money" is a very old premise (freshness).
- 1 vs 6: 1 wins — ex-joke is stock (freshness).
- 1 vs 7: 1 wins — smoke-alarm-when-cooking is a common observation; 1's turn is more absurd and specific.

## Winner

Candidate 1. Specific person (my dentist), concrete scene (grinding teeth at
night), turn in the last third (charging rent to a construction crew), open
loop (why is there a crew in my jaw?), no preaching. The grinding =
construction noise metaphor is internally coherent. Two-sentence beat gives
the punch a pause. 117 characters, single line, UTF-8, non-blank — gate passes.
